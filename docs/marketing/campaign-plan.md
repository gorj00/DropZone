# DropZone launch campaign plan

Written 5 November 2026. Covers the 12 weeks after production access on Google Play.

DropZone will also be released on iPhone. The date is not set, so the plan starts on Android and switches to the steps marked "once the iPhone version is live" on the day the App Store version is available.

Numbers marked "estimate" are starting guesses from public benchmarks, not DropZone data. Replace them with real figures after the first four weeks.

## 1. Campaign overview

- **Name:** "Locked until you move"
- **Summary:** show Gen Z and millennials, in short person-led videos, that DropZone locks their socials until today's quests are done, and turn the installs into paying PRO subscribers.
- **Primary objective:** 30 paying subscribers by 14 February 2027 (stretch: 60).
- **Secondary objectives:**
  - 1,500 installs in 12 weeks.
  - Learn the real cost per paying subscriber, so the budget can grow from profits.
  - A public Play rating of 4.3 or higher from at least 20 ratings.

Awareness is not a separate goal. It is the top of the same funnel: views → installs → finished onboarding (the 7-day PRO trial starts here) → paid subscription.

### Timing

- **Now to 22 November (preparation):** the closed test ends around 14 November, then Google reviews the production application.
- **Week 1 starts when production access is granted.** The dates below assume Monday 23 November. If approval comes later, every week shifts by the same amount.
- **Length: 12 weeks, to 14 February 2027.** This is long enough to cover the New Year's resolution season, which is the strongest period of the year for weight-loss and fitness apps, and to see a full month of renewals before deciding on a bigger budget.
- **iPhone release:** if it is live before 28 December, the New Year push covers both platforms. If it comes later, it gets its own small launch in the week it ships (see the calendar).

## 2. Target audience

### Where to target: USA only, or global?

It depends on the platform, so the answer changes when the iPhone version ships.

**While DropZone is Android-only:** English content for everyone, and paid ads only for Android users in the USA, UK, Canada and Australia.

- **Why not USA only:** young Americans mostly use iPhones. In a 2024 Bloomberg Intelligence survey, 79% of US 18–24-year-olds preferred Apple. Across all ages, 55–60% of US phones are iPhones. A US-only campaign aimed at Gen Z would mostly reach people who cannot install the app yet.
- **Why not the whole world for paid ads:** installs in low-income markets are cheap (often under $0.30 on Android) but those users rarely pay $8.99 a month. Cheap installs that never subscribe waste the budget.
- **Why the USA still gets the biggest share of paid spend:** North America converts installs to trials better than other regions (7.1% against 5.7% in Asia-Pacific in RevenueCat's 2026 data, as quoted by secondary sources), and US Android installs are affordable ($0.75–2.00 on Google App campaigns, vendor estimate).
**Once the iPhone version is live:** the USA becomes the main paid market, and most of the paid budget goes to iPhone users there.

- Young Americans are mostly on iPhone, so the core audience becomes reachable.
- iPhone installs cost about twice as much as Android installs (worldwide averages of $2.24 against $1.12 in 2026, Adjust figures quoted by Searchlab). iPhone users generally spend more on subscriptions, which was not checked for this category, so the cost per paying subscriber is what decides.
- Android ads continue in the UK, Canada and Australia at a lower level, and in the USA if the first weeks showed that they pay back.

**On both platforms:**

- **Organic video needs no targeting.** English videos are distributed worldwide by the platforms. Installs from other countries are free, so they are welcome.
- **Review by country after week 4, and again four weeks after the iPhone release.** Move paid budget to the country and platform with the lowest cost per finished onboarding.

What the research did not find: a reliable 2026 table of install cost or revenue per install by country for Android health and fitness apps, and a current figure for Gen Z iPhone share in the US. The figures above come from vendor blogs and one 2024 survey.

### Primary segment

People aged 18–34 in English-speaking countries who want to lose weight and spend hours a day on TikTok, Instagram or YouTube. Android users first, iPhone users once that version is live. (The app is 18+.)

- **Pain points:** starts a diet or routine and quits within two weeks; "no willpower"; doomscrolling instead of training; guilt about screen time.
- **Motivation:** wants an outside force that makes skipping impossible, and wants it to feel like a game.
- **Where they are:** TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Reddit (r/getdisciplined, r/nosurf, r/digitalminimalism).
- **Buying stage:** aware of the problem, not aware that an app can lock their socials behind a workout.

### Secondary segment

People aged 18–34 who want to build muscle through daily habits (the "Build muscle" mode). Same channels, plus gym and calisthenics content. Same message, with strength quests in place of weight-loss quests.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** your socials stay locked until you've done today's quests.

| Supporting message | Proof in the app |
|---|---|
| You don't need willpower. The lock does the work. | App lock: the chosen apps stay locked until quests are done, or unlock by minutes per quest. |
| It's a game, so you keep going. | Daily quests, streaks, milestones with a reward photo, leaderboards. |
| "Trust me, I did it" doesn't count. | AI rep counter for squats and push-ups, scale reading from a photo, friends verify proofs. |
| Your journey can stay private. | Sharing is optional; AI can check milestones instead of friends. |

**Tone:** dry and ironic, the same voice as the store listing ("Fair? No. Effective? Yes.").

**By channel:**

- **TikTok, Reels, Shorts:** a person, not the app, is the subject. The app appears only as the thing that blocks them. No voice-over that sounds like an advert. One fast take. Captions say "on Android, iPhone coming soon" until the iPhone version is live.
- **Reddit:** honest founder voice. "I built this because I kept scrolling instead of training." No slogans.
- **Play listing and paid ads:** the short description as written, plus the lock screen as the first image.

**Claims to avoid everywhere:** specific weight-loss results ("lose 5 kg in a month"), before/after bodies in paid ads, and any testimonial from a person who doesn't exist (see Risks).

## 4. Channel strategy

### Owned (free)

| Channel | Why | Format | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts | Where the audience already scrolls; the product's hook is visual and funny. One video is posted to all three. | 15–30 s person-led videos, 3–4 a week | High |
| Play Store listing | Every install passes through it. Small gains here multiply everything else. | Listing experiments (built into Play Console): first screenshot, short description | Low |
| dropzone.mobi | A place to send people from Reddit and video bios; holds the store link. | Existing site, store button with a tracked link | Low |

### Earned (free or nearly free)

| Channel | Why | Format | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reddit | The audience describes this exact problem there every day. | One founder post per subreddit, then genuine replies in relevant threads. Read each subreddit's self-promotion rules first. | Medium |
| Small creators | A real person locked out of their own phone is more convincing than any advert. | Free lifetime PRO in exchange for one honest video, marked as gifted | Medium |
| Friends inside the app | Friend verification makes every user a reason for a friend to install. | "Challenge a friend" as a recurring video theme | Low |

### Paid

Each paid service is optional. Without any of them the plan still runs on organic video and Reddit, only slower.

**Google App campaigns (Google Ads)**

- **Why:** it shows the app only to Android users (Search, Play, YouTube), so none of that budget reaches people who can't install it, and it counts installs through Google Play without adding code to the app.
- **What breaks without it:** nothing. Installs come only from organic content.
- **Optional:** yes.
- **Cost:** $5–8 a day, so $150–250 a month. Countries: USA, UK, Canada, Australia.

**Apple Search Ads (once the iPhone version is live)**

- **Why:** it shows DropZone at the top of App Store search results to iPhone users who are already looking for a weight-loss, habit or screen-time app, and the USA is its strongest market.
- **What breaks without it:** nothing. iPhone installs come only from organic content and App Store search.
- **Optional:** yes.
- **Cost:** about $5 a day to start, so about $150 a month, taken from the existing budget. Vendor estimates put health and fitness installs at $1.50–8 each.

**TikTok Spark Ads (TikTok Ads Manager)**

- **Why:** it puts money behind a video that has already done well organically, so the budget only goes to proven creatives.
- **What breaks without it:** nothing. Good videos reach fewer people.
- **Optional:** yes.
- **Cost:** about $20 a day for three days per boost, so about $60 each; one or two boosts a month. (TikTok's minimum daily budget is from memory, not checked.)

**Paid creator videos**

- **Why:** more real faces than free lifetime PRO alone will attract.
- **What breaks without it:** nothing.
- **Optional:** yes, and only from month 2.
- **Cost:** $40–80 per video from small creators.

Meta ads are left out for now. Splitting $250–500 across more ad platforms would leave each one too little data to learn from.

## 7. Success metrics

| Metric | Target by 14 Feb (estimate) | Where to read it |
|---|---|---|
| **Paying subscribers (primary)** | 30, stretch 60 | RevenueCat |
| Installs | 1,500 (about 600 paid, 900 organic) | Play Console; App Store Connect once on iPhone |
| Finished onboarding (trial started) | 60% of installs | Backoffice |
| Trial to paid | 3.5% of finished onboardings | RevenueCat |
| Cost per paying subscriber (all spend ÷ new subscribers) | Under $40 by week 9, heading towards $20 | Ad spend and RevenueCat |
| Video views, all platforms | 300,000 | TikTok, Instagram, YouTube analytics |
| Play rating | 4.3 or higher from 20+ ratings | Play Console |

- **Why trial-to-paid is set low:** the trial starts without a card, for everyone who finishes onboarding. Such trials convert far below the 35–40% that card-up-front trials reach in health and fitness apps.
- **Review rhythm:** 30 minutes every Monday, plus the three larger reviews in weeks 4, 9 and 12.
- **iPhone:** the targets assume Android only. Once the iPhone version is live, read every metric per platform (RevenueCat shows both stores), and raise the subscriber target after its first four weeks of data.

## 8. Budget allocation

| | Month 1 (weeks 1–4) | Month 2 (weeks 5–8, New Year) | Month 3 (weeks 9–12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google App campaigns | $150 | $250 | $150–250 |
| TikTok boosts | $60 | $120 | $60–120 |
| Paid creator videos | $0 | $80 | $0–80 |
| Reserve (about 10–15%) | $40 | $50 | $40–50 |
| **Total** | **$250** | **$500** | **$250–500** |

Video production is not in the table; it is done with the tools already in use.

**Once the iPhone version is live,** keep the monthly total and split it roughly: Apple Search Ads 40%, Google App campaigns 30%, TikTok boosts 20%, reserve 10%. TikTok boosts can then target both platforms.

**Rule for growing the budget from profits:** raise paid spend only when the cost per paying subscriber is below what a subscriber brings in over six months, read from RevenueCat. As a rough guide, a monthly subscriber pays $8.99, about $7.64 after Google's 15% fee, so one who stays three months brings about $23. Until real numbers exist, treat $20 per subscriber as the limit for scaling. Apple also keeps 15% if the developer account is in its Small Business Program, and 30% otherwise.

The prices used here ($2.99 weekly, $8.99 monthly, $59.99 yearly) are taken from the app's test data. Check them against Play Console.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Android only at launch.** Much of the young US audience is on iPhone and cannot install until the iPhone version ships. | Until then, paid ads go to Android users only and captions say "on Android, iPhone coming soon". |
| **The lock on iPhone.** The app lock is the core promise. On iPhone it depends on Apple's Screen Time permission, which Apple has to approve for the app (to be confirmed during iPhone development). | Don't announce or advertise the iPhone version until the lock works there. If it works differently, the videos must show what really happens. |
| **Ad and platform rules.** Weight-loss adverts are restricted on TikTok and Meta, and realistic AI-generated people must be labelled. A made-up person claiming results is a fake testimonial. | Paid creatives use the phone-lock angle, not weight-loss promises. AI-made videos carry the platform's AI label and are skits, never "I lost 10 kg" stories. Real results come only from real users. |
| **Paid installs don't pay back.** With a no-card trial and a small budget, the first weeks will probably cost more per subscriber than a subscriber brings in. | Treat month 1 as paying to learn. Apply the scaling rule in section 8. Keep organic video as the main engine. |
| **One person, many videos.** 45 videos in 12 weeks is a lot alongside development. | Produce in batches (prep weeks and week 5). The floor is 3 videos a week; daily posting only in weeks 6–7. |
| **Too little data per ad platform.** | One always-on platform per store (Google for Android, Apple Search Ads for iPhone) and short TikTok boosts. |

## 10. Next steps

1. Create the TikTok, Instagram and YouTube accounts and reserve the DropZone name.
2. Produce and schedule the first 9 videos.
3. Send the production application when the closed test ends (around 14 November).
4. Create the Google Ads account, link it to Play Console and prepare the App campaign (start it only in week 1).
5. Make one tracked Play Store link per channel.
6. Prepare listing experiment 1 (alternative first screenshot).

**Decisions that are yours:**

- Whether to run paid ads from week 1 or wait for the week 4 review.
- Whether small creators get free lifetime PRO.
- Whether to lower prices in lower-income countries (Play Console allows a price per country), since organic videos will bring installs from them.
- The iPhone release date. The plan does not assume one.
